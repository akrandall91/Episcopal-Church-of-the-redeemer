A living legacy

1906

Faith carried forward.

Rooted in a 1906 mission vision for Black Episcopalians in Greensboro, Redeemer has remained a spiritual home and civic witness through generations of city history.

Explore our history
  1. 1906

    The mission takes root

    The Diocese identifies the need for a mission serving Black Episcopalians in Greensboro.

  2. Mid-century

    Faith and civic witness

    Redeemer serves as a spiritual anchor through Greensboro's civil rights era.

  3. 1979

    Full parish status

    A milestone in a growing Episcopal presence and enduring ministry.

  4. 2026

    Preservation work

    Greensboro's Civil Rights preservation project helps document Redeemer's witness for the National Register process.

    The next chapter

    Restoring sacred space for worship, access, service, and future generations.

Faith in the neighborhood

A parish with a civic memory and a present calling.

Redeemer’s official history records worship beginning at the A&T campus chapel in June 1909 and the church serving as a meeting place for Civil Rights organizers during desegregation. That legacy invites continuing partnership, care, and bridge-building in Greensboro.
